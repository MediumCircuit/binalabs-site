Can your setup pay for the broker?

Enter your spread, your slippage and your stop. The tool tells you what fraction of your planned risk the broker takes before your strategy has done anything at all. No signup, no email, nothing installed.

Same units for all four. On Exness: 1 point = 0.00001 for 5-digit FX, 0.001 for JPY pairs and gold.

The rule, and where it came from

A setup has to survive a round trip of spread + 2 × slippage. If that is more than roughly 20% of the planned risk, the edge is being paid to the broker.

Cost as a share of riskWhat it means
< 0.15Comfortable. Costs are a rounding error.
0.15 – 0.27Workable, but every pip of edge has to be real.
> 0.27Break-even. The strategy must be right often enough to cover the haircut.
> 0.50More than half the risk is gone before the trade closes.

The 0.27 break-even comes from a linear fit across seven symbol/timeframe combinations of one order-block strategy (expectancy = 0.212 − 0.773 × cost/risk, R² = 0.961, r = −0.98). Across twelve combinations the slope is steeper: 0.32 − 1.44 × cost/risk, R² = 0.64. The direction of the relationship is the robust part; the exact coefficients are specific to that strategy and should not be treated as a universal law.

Why we built this

We tested 35 hypotheses on real Exness bars with real spreads and real swap. Twenty-three died. Two came close and both failed for the same reason: the trades looked independent but were not. The single most common reason a strategy that looks good on paper loses money is not the signal — it is that the stop is too small for the cost of trading it.

If this tool says your setup is in trouble, that is worth knowing before you buy the indicator. If it says your setup is fine, the next question is whether the signal actually carries information. That is what an audit answers.
